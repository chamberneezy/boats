# Lake Brienz boat trips and timetable

This guide covers Lake Brienz boat trips and the timetable: where the boats go, how long they take, how often they run in summer and in the late season, and which tickets and passes are valid. Lake Brienz, the Brienzersee, is a turquoise mountain lake in the Bernese Oberland between Interlaken and the village of Brienz, and its boats link the two with lakeside villages and the Giessbach falls. Unlike Lake Thun, it has no boats in the middle of winter. Lacus puts every pier and every boat on the lake into one search, from the official timetable.

## How the boats work

Every regular timetable boat on Lake Brienz is run by BLS Schifffahrt AG, which calls itself BLS Lake Cruise in English and also runs the boats on Lake Thun. Boats have linked Interlaken and Brienz since 1839, and BLS has run them since 1913. Its Lake Brienz fleet is four boats, among them the paddle steamer Lötschberg.

The boats run from spring to early December: from 3 April to 6 December 2026, and from 26 March to 5 December 2027. For 2026–27 the periods are:

The piers at Interlaken Ost and Brienz are a few minutes' walk from the railway stations of the same name.

Interlaken has two piers on two different lakes. Lake Brienz boats use Interlaken Ost. Lake Thun boats use Interlaken West, at the other end of the town. No boat runs between the two lakes: you take the train between Interlaken Ost and Interlaken West.

There is one route, along the whole lake from Interlaken Ost to Brienz, in about 1 h 15 min. Boats call at Bönigen, Iseltwald and Giessbach, and some also at Ringgenberg, Niederried or Oberried am Brienzersee, so not every boat stops at every pier.

## Classic combinations

- **Giessbach falls:** the falls drop more than 500 m down the rock face into the lake. From the Giessbach pier, a path leads up to the falls and under them, and the Giessbach funicular, opened in 1879, runs from the pier to the Grandhotel Giessbach, about 100 m above the lake. BLS calls the funicular the oldest in Europe. It also sells a combined ticket with the boat and lunch at the hotel.
- **Iseltwald to Giessbach on foot:** a lakeside path through the forest, about an hour and a half, with the boat for the other way. Iseltwald's own pier became well known from the Korean series «Crash Landing on You».
- **Brienz Rothorn:** from Brienz, Switzerland's oldest steam cog railway goes up the Rothorn.
- **Boat and train:** boat from Interlaken Ost to Brienz, about 1 h 15 min, and the train back to Interlaken Ost, about 20 minutes. A round trip by boat takes about 3 hours, with a 20-minute stop in Brienz.
- **Lake Thun:** at Interlaken, take the train from Interlaken Ost to Interlaken West for the Lake Thun boats. BLS's day ticket for both lakes includes that train.

## The boats

BLS's Lake Brienz fleet has four boats: the paddle steamer Lötschberg and the motor ships Brienz, Jungfrau and Interlaken.

The Lötschberg is a Belle Époque saloon steamer built in 1914 by Escher Wyss in Zürich, for up to 800 passengers. It entered service in 1914, but was taken out of service after only nine days when the First World War broke out. Its restoration in 2000/01 won the ICOMOS Special Award in 2008. It runs in the summer months. In BLS's published boat plan for 2026, it ran two round trips a day from 23 May to 27 September, every day except Mondays and Tuesdays, leaving Interlaken Ost at 11:07 and 14:07 and Brienz at 12:40 and 15:40, plus the Saturday evening boat. BLS notes that the boat plan can change at short notice.

All BLS boats except the Interlaken, the smallest, are wheelchair accessible, with level access to the outside decks and second-class restaurants, and an accessible toilet.

## Tickets and passes

On BLS's regular timetable boats, these tickets and passes are valid for free travel, in the class they were bought for:

- GA travelcard
- Swiss Travel Pass, Swiss Travel Pass Flex (with the date entered) and Swiss Transfer Ticket
- Seepass, BLS's annual first-class pass for Lake Thun and Lake Brienz
- Day passes for Half Fare holders, Saver Day Passes, municipal day tickets and children's day passes
- Berner Oberland Pass and Jungfrau Travel Pass
- Eurail and Interrail passes, with the date entered
- Junior Card and Children's Co-travelcard, for children under 16 travelling with the cardholder

The Half Fare travelcard gives 50% off. Children aged 6 to 15 pay half price, and children under 6 travel free. There are no discounts for seniors. The Tell-Pass is not valid. With a second-class pass or ticket, first class needs a supplement, which you can buy at the pier or on board.

BLS sells two day tickets in 2026. The day ticket for Lake Brienz alone costs CHF 75 in second class and CHF 112 in first class, or CHF 37.50 and CHF 56 with the Half Fare travelcard. The day ticket for both lakes costs CHF 83 and CHF 125, or CHF 41.50 and CHF 62.50 with the Half Fare travelcard, and includes the train between Interlaken Ost and Interlaken West and the buses in Interlaken. On your birthday, BLS gives you a free first-class day ticket when you show an identity card or passport.

You can buy tickets on board, from spring to autumn at BLS's ticket offices at the piers in Interlaken Ost and Brienz, online in BLS's shop, and at most public transport ticket offices in Switzerland. On board you can pay in Swiss francs or euros, with TWINT or by card. BLS also runs themed cruises, such as breakfast and burger boats; the GA, the Half Fare travelcard and the Seepass are not valid on them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I buy a ticket in Lacus?**
No. Lacus doesn't sell tickets or anything else. You buy your ticket from BLS, the boat operator, online in its shop; Lacus links each trip to BLS's ticket page.

**Do the boats on Lake Brienz run in winter?**
Not in the middle of winter. The 2026 season ends on 6 December, and there are no boats until 26 March 2027. From 12 October to 6 December 2026, two boats a day run each way between Interlaken Ost and Brienz.

**Is the Lake Brienz boat included in the Swiss Travel Pass?**
Yes. The Swiss Travel Pass and the Swiss Travel Pass Flex are valid on all regular timetable boats, in the class of the pass.

**Is the boat included in the GA travelcard or the Half Fare travelcard?**
The GA is valid for free travel on all regular boats, in its class. The Half Fare travelcard gives 50% off.

**How long is the boat from Interlaken to Brienz?**
About 1 h 15 min from Interlaken Ost, with stops at piers along the way. In the summer 2026 timetable there are 5 direct boats a day, and one more on summer Saturday evenings. In the autumn and winter timetable there are 2.

**How do I get to the Giessbach falls by boat?**
Take the boat to the Giessbach pier: about 1 hour from Interlaken Ost or about 10 minutes from Brienz. From the pier, walk up to the falls or take the funicular to the Grandhotel Giessbach.

**Which pier in Interlaken do the Lake Brienz boats use?**
Interlaken Ost. Interlaken West is the pier for Lake Thun. Take the train between the two stations; BLS's day ticket for both lakes includes it.

**Can I take my dog or my bike on the boat?**
Yes. Dogs under 30 cm at the shoulder travel free in a suitable basket or bag. Other dogs need a dog ticket at half the second-class fare, at most CHF 15 for a day; the SBB dog day pass and dog GA are also valid. Dogs are allowed inside and in the restaurants. Bikes travel if there is space, also at half fare and at most CHF 15 a day; SBB bike day passes and annual bike passes are valid. Bike places cannot be reserved.
