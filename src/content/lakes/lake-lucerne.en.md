# Lake Lucerne boat trips and timetable

This guide covers Lake Lucerne boat trips and the timetable: where the boats go, how long they take, how often they run in summer and in winter, and which tickets and passes are valid. Lake Lucerne, the Vierwaldstättersee, is a lake of several long arms in central Switzerland, and its regular boats link Luzern with lakeside villages, mountain railways and the Rütli meadow. Lacus puts every pier and every boat on the lake into one search, from the official timetable.

## How the boats work

Every regular timetable boat on Lake Lucerne, apart from the Beckenried–Gersau car ferry, is run by one company, the Schifffahrtsgesellschaft des Vierwaldstättersees (SGV), the Lake Lucerne Navigation Company. Its fleet is five historic paddle steamers and 14 motor ships.

The boats run all year, but the timetable changes with the seasons. For 2026–27 the periods are:

| Period | Dates |
|---|---|
| Summer | 23 May – 6 September 2026 |
| Autumn | 7 September – 18 October 2026 |
| Winter | 19 October 2026 – 16 April 2027 |
| Spring | 17 April – 21 May 2027 |

In Luzern, the boats leave from the lakefront opposite the railway station. They use three numbered piers:

- Pier 1: Weggis, Vitznau (for the Rigi), Brunnen and Flüelen
- Pier 2: Alpnachstad (for Pilatus)
- Pier 3: Küssnacht am Rigi and Bürgenstock

![Luzern, the Chapel Bridge on the river Reuss](photo:lake-lucerne-day)

The longest regular route runs from Luzern along the whole lake to Flüelen, at the southern end. On the way it calls at Weggis, Vitznau, Beckenried, Gersau, Treib, Brunnen and the Rütli. Shorter routes go south-west to Hergiswil, Stansstad and Alpnachstad, north-east to Küssnacht am Rigi, and across to Kehrsiten-Bürgenstock.

A car ferry links Beckenried and Gersau across the lake in about 20 minutes. It also takes foot passengers and bicycles. A separate company, Autofähre Vierwaldstättersee, runs it, not SGV. It runs roughly every hour from spring to autumn and stops for the winter. The 2027 season runs from 26 March to 10 October 2027. Tickets for the ferry are sold only on board.

## Classic combinations

- **Rigi:** the Rigi cogwheel railway starts about 50 m from the Vitznau pier. From the Weggis pier it is about 900 m to the aerial cableway to Rigi Kaltbad.
- **Pilatus, the Golden Round Trip:** boat from Luzern (pier 2) to Alpnachstad, the cogwheel railway to the top of Pilatus, then gondolas and a cableway down to Kriens and the bus back to Luzern, or the same in reverse. Pilatus offers it from May to October. The cogwheel railway is about 200 m from the Alpnachstad pier.
- **Bürgenstock:** boat from Luzern to Kehrsiten-Bürgenstock, then the Bürgenstock funicular up to the Bürgenstock Resort.
- **Rütli:** the meadow where, by tradition, the Swiss Confederation was founded has its own pier. Boats call there from Luzern, Brunnen and Flüelen.

## The boats

SGV's five paddle steamers were built between 1901 and 1928:

| Steamer | First trip |
|---|---|
| Uri | 8 May 1901 |
| Unterwalden | 18 May 1902 |
| Schiller | 21 May 1906 |
| Gallia | 23 July 1913 |
| Stadt Luzern | 23 June 1928 |

Stadt Luzern is SGV's flagship. Most of the steamers run only in the warmer months, from spring to autumn. Uri and Unterwalden have been adapted for winter, with an enclosed restaurant on the upper deck, and also run a festive steamer programme during the holidays in late December and early January.

The steamers run regular timetable trips, not only special cruises. In September and October 2026, SGV put at least one paddle steamer on regular trips every day. In SGV's published boat plan from 19 October to 21 December 2026, the winter timetable, motor ships run every regular trip, and the Unterwalden runs special cruises on some Fridays and Saturdays.

The 14 motor ships include the Diamant, the Bürgenstock, the Saphir, the Europa, the Gotthard and the small electric Rütli. Every SGV boat except the Rütli has full wheelchair access and an accessible toilet. On the Diamant, a lift goes up to first class on the upper deck. ## Tickets and passes

On all of SGV's regular timetable trips, these tickets and passes are valid for free travel:

- GA travelcard
- Swiss Travel Pass, Swiss Travel Pass Flex and Swiss Transfer Ticket
- Tell-Pass / Lucerne Travel Pass
- Lake Lucerne Pass (SGV's annual pass)
- Day tickets bought with the Half Fare travelcard, and municipal day tickets
- Junior Card, for children under 16 travelling with a parent who holds one

These give 50% off: the Half Fare travelcard, Interrail and Eurail passes. Children aged 6 to 16 pay half price, and children under 6 travel free. There are no discounts for seniors.

The panorama cruise on the yacht Saphir is an exception: it needs a special ticket. The GA and the Swiss Travel Pass give a discount on it, and public transport day passes are not valid. The passes above apply to regular timetable trips, not to special cruises.

You can buy tickets on board without a surcharge, but buy one right after you board. SGV has ticket offices all year at its piers in Luzern and Weggis, and from spring to autumn in Vitznau and Brunnen. Tickets are also sold online, and a ticket bought online can be shown as a QR code on a phone. On board you can pay in Swiss francs or euros, with TWINT or by card.

A route ticket lets you get off and back on along the way. For example, a Luzern–Rütli–Luzern ticket lets you break the trip at any pier, as long as you stay on the same route.

## Special cruises

Besides the timetable, SGV runs special cruises such as brunch, fondue and New Year's Eve evenings. Recent examples include the Fondue and Raclette Steamboat, the Brunch Steamer and the New Year's Eve Gala Cruise. Lacus lists these special cruises, with their dates, boats and times, in the app.

- The lake has several arms, so a boat from Luzern to Flüelen changes direction again and again.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I buy a ticket in Lacus?**
No. Lacus doesn't sell tickets or anything else. You buy your ticket from SGV, the boat operator, in its online shop; Lacus links each trip to that shop, already filled in with the trip.

**Do I need to book a Lake Lucerne boat trip in advance?**
No. You do not need a reservation for the open decks, and you cannot reserve seats there. You can buy your ticket on board without a surcharge. Tables in the onboard restaurants can be reserved.

**Is the Lake Lucerne boat included in the Swiss Travel Pass?**
Yes. The Swiss Travel Pass and the Swiss Travel Pass Flex are valid for free travel on all regular timetable trips. The Saphir panorama cruise is not included, though the pass gives a discount on it.

**Is the boat included in the GA travelcard or the Half Fare travelcard?**
The GA is valid for free travel on all regular trips. The Half Fare travelcard gives 50% off.

**Do the boats on Lake Lucerne run in winter?**
Yes. SGV runs boats every day of the year, with a smaller winter timetable from 19 October 2026 to 16 April 2027. On a winter weekday there are 8 direct boats from Luzern to Vitznau and one to Flüelen, and the winter boats are heated.

**How long is the boat from Luzern to Flüelen?**
About 2 h 45 min on a direct boat, with stops at piers along the whole lake. In the summer 2026 timetable there are 7 direct boats a day. In winter there is one.

**How long is the boat from Luzern to Vitznau?**
Most direct boats take about 1 hour, with stops at Verkehrshaus, Hertenstein and Weggis. The fastest boat of the day takes about 45 minutes.

**Can I take my dog on the boat?**
Yes. Small animals under 30 cm at the shoulder travel free in a suitable container. Other dogs need a dog ticket (CHF 10 one way, CHF 12 return) or a dog day pass or GA. Dogs are allowed in the restaurants on regular trips but not on themed cruises. Guide dogs are always allowed.

**Can I take my bike on the boat?**
Yes, if there is space. A bike ticket costs CHF 10 one way or CHF 12 return. Bike tickets and passes from SBB and other Swiss transport companies are not valid on Lake Lucerne. Groups with more than five bikes must book in advance.
