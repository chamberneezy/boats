# Lake Geneva boat trips and timetable

This guide covers Lake Geneva boat trips and the timetable: where the boats go, how long they take, how often they run in summer and in winter, and which tickets and passes are valid. Lake Geneva, the Lac Léman, is shared by Switzerland and France, and its boats link Geneva, Lausanne and the Montreux Riviera with lakeside towns on both shores. Lacus puts every CGN pier and boat on the lake into one search, from the official timetable.

## How the boats work

The boats along and across Lake Geneva are run by one company, the Compagnie Générale de Navigation sur le lac Léman (CGN). Its fleet includes eight historic Belle Époque paddle boats. The small boats in Geneva's harbour belong to a separate company (see below).

CGN runs two kinds of service. The lake lines are the longer trips along the shore, mostly from spring to autumn. The CGN-Mobilité lines are short, frequent routes across the lake that run all year:

- N1: Lausanne-Ouchy – Evian
- N2: Lausanne-Ouchy – Thonon
- N3: Nyon – Yvoire
- N4: Corsier – Bellevue, across the Geneva end of the lake

The timetable changes with the seasons. In the 2026 timetable, the periods are:

| Period | Dates |
|---|---|
| Lake lines every day | 25 April – 11 October 2026 |
| Winter | 12 October – 12 December 2026 |

The lake lines fall into three parts. In the east, boats link Lausanne-Ouchy with Lutry, Cully, Vevey, Montreux, Chillon, Villeneuve, Le Bouveret and St-Gingolph. In the west, boats link Geneva with Nyon, Yvoire, Rolle and Morges, and some go on to Lausanne-Ouchy. A third line makes round trips from Geneva Mont-Blanc along the shore to Bellevue.

In Geneva, most boats leave from the Mont-Blanc pier, on the Quai du Mont-Blanc; a few use Jardin-Anglais, on the Promenade du Lac, and Eaux-Vives. In Lausanne, the pier is at Ouchy, on the lakefront.

The Mouettes genevoises, the small boats in the Geneva harbour, are a separate company. They run four lines, M1 to M4, all year, between Molard, Pâquis, Eaux-Vives, Port Noir and De-Chateaubriand, and they are part of Geneva's unireso fare network. Lacus does not include them.

## Classic combinations

- **Chillon Castle:** the castle is about 2 minutes on foot along the lakefront from the Chillon pier. CGN offers 15% off the castle entrance ticket when you combine it with a boat trip.
- **Lavaux:** the Lavaux Express, a small tourist train through the vineyards, leaves from the Lutry pier.
- **Chaplin's World:** from the Vevey pier it is about 7 minutes on foot to the Vevey, Poste bus stop, then bus 212 to the Chaplin stop. CGN offers 20% off the entrance ticket combined with a boat trip.
- **Olympic Museum:** about 10 minutes on foot along the lakefront from the Lausanne-Ouchy pier.
- **Geneva Water Fountain:** about 10 minutes on foot along the lakefront from the Jardin-Anglais pier.
- **The French shore:** Evian, Thonon, Yvoire and Nernier are in France. To enter France, Swiss and EU citizens need a valid identity card or passport, and others may also need a visa.

## The boats

CGN's eight Belle Époque paddle boats were built between 1904 and 1927. Five are steamers:

| Steamer | Commissioned |
|---|---|
| Montreux | 1904 |
| La Suisse | 1910 |
| Savoie | 1914 |
| Simplon | 1915–1920 |
| Rhône | 1927 |

The other three, Vevey (1907), Italie (1908) and Helvétie (1926), keep their paddle wheels but are driven by diesel-electric engines. La Suisse and Montreux usually run from mid-April to mid-October. Rhône, Savoie, Vevey and Italie run all year.

The paddle boats run regular timetable trips, not only special cruises. From 27 September to 11 October 2026, according to CGN's departure boards, the steamers Montreux and Rhône and the Vevey ran regular trips every day. On the first winter Sundays, 18 and 25 October 2026, the Vevey ran the trips from Geneva.

The motor ships include the Lausanne, the largest, for 1,200 passengers, and the Évian-les-Bains, a hybrid boat that entered service in 2024, as well as the Léman, Henry-Dunant, Général-Guisan and Ville-de-Genève. The small Morges, Lavaux and Valais take 200 passengers each, and the Valais runs line N4. Every CGN boat has a toilet for disabled people, and wheelchair access is usually possible but depends on the boat and how busy it is. ## Tickets and passes

On CGN's boats, these tickets and passes are valid, except on events:

- GA travelcard
- Swiss Travel Pass
- Day passes issued by Swiss municipalities
- Junior Card and Grandchild Card, for children aged 6 to 16

The Half Fare travelcard gives 50% off. Children aged 6 and over pay half price, and children under 6 travel free. There are no reduced fares for seniors or students. Regional public transport passes, such as Mobilis and unireso, are not valid on CGN's boats.

You can buy tickets at CGN's ticket offices or on board, at no extra cost. The ticket offices at Lausanne-Ouchy and Geneva Mont-Blanc stay open in winter. Tickets are also sold online, and combined train and boat tickets are sold at SBB stations. A ticket is valid for one day, until 05:00 the next morning, so if you miss a boat you can take the next one. Buying online does not reserve a seat.

The boats have first and second class. First class gives access to the whole boat, including the upper decks. With a second-class ticket you can use the dining areas during meal times; outside meal times, the main-deck dining room and the upper deck are for first class only. You can upgrade on board.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I buy a ticket in Lacus?**
No. Lacus doesn't sell tickets or anything else. You buy your ticket from CGN, the boat operator, in its online shop; Lacus links each trip to that shop, already filled in with the trip.

**Do I need to book a Lake Geneva boat trip in advance?**
No. You can buy your ticket on board or at a ticket office at no extra cost. Seats are not numbered, so buying online does not guarantee a seat.

**Is the Lake Geneva boat included in the Swiss Travel Pass?**
Yes. CGN accepts the Swiss Travel Pass on all its trips except events.

**Is the boat included in the GA travelcard or the Half Fare travelcard?**
The GA is valid on all CGN trips except events. The Half Fare travelcard gives 50% off.

**Do the boats on Lake Geneva run in winter?**
Yes. In the winter timetable, from 12 October 2026, the N lines keep running: on a winter weekday there are 15 boats from Lausanne-Ouchy to Evian and 13 from Nyon to Yvoire. The longer lake lines run on Sundays only.

**How long is the boat from Lausanne to Evian?**
About 35 minutes, on line N1. On a weekday there are 15 boats a day in each direction, in summer and in winter.

**Do I need a passport for the boat to Evian or Yvoire?**
These piers are in France. Swiss and EU citizens need a valid identity card or passport, and other travellers may also need a visa.

**Can I take my dog on the boat?**
Yes. Dogs generally need a second-class half-fare ticket. Small dogs, no more than 30 cm at the shoulder, travel free in a basket or bag.

**Can I take my bike on the boat?**
Yes, on most trips. A bike ticket costs CHF 15 and is valid for the whole day. Boats generally take no more than 10 bikes, and bikes are not allowed on some cruises.
