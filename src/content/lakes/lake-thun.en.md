# Lake Thun boat trips and timetable

This guide covers Lake Thun boat trips and the timetable: where the boats go, how long they take, how often they run in summer and in winter, and which tickets and passes are valid. Lake Thun, the Thunersee, lies in the Bernese Oberland between the town of Thun and Interlaken, and its regular boats link the two, calling at Spiez, Oberhofen, the St. Beatus Caves and other lakeside villages on the way. Lacus puts every pier and every boat on the lake into one search, from the official timetable.

## How the boats work

Every regular timetable boat on Lake Thun is run by BLS Schifffahrt AG, which calls itself BLS Lake Cruise in English and also runs the boats on Lake Brienz. BLS has run the boats on both lakes since 1913. Its Lake Thun fleet is eight boats, among them the paddle steamer Blümlisalp.

The boats run all year on Lake Thun, but the timetable changes with the seasons. For 2026–27 the periods are:

In Thun, the boats leave from the pier beside the railway station, on a canal that leads out to the lake. At the other end, Interlaken West pier is next to Interlaken West station, at the end of a ship canal about 2.75 km long that was built in the 1890s. In Spiez, the pier is down by the lake, about a 15-minute walk from the station.

Interlaken has two piers on two different lakes. Lake Thun boats use Interlaken West. Lake Brienz boats use Interlaken Ost, at the other end of the town. No boat runs between the two lakes: you take the train between Interlaken West and Interlaken Ost.

The main route runs the length of the lake, from Thun to Interlaken West in about 2 h 10 min. Most boats call at Hünibach, Hilterfingen, Oberhofen, Gunten, Spiez, Faulensee, Merligen, Beatenbucht, the St. Beatus Caves and Neuhaus on the way. A few boats call at Gwatt Deltapark, Einigen and Leissigen instead.

## Classic combinations

- **St. Beatus Caves:** the Beatushöhlen-Sundlauenen pier is the stop for the caves, where a lit path leads through the cave world of the Niederhorn massif. BLS sells a combined ticket with the boat and admission to the caves.
- **Niederhorn:** boat from Thun to Beatenbucht, then on up to the Niederhorn. BLS sells a combined ticket from Thun with the boat, bus and mountain railway, there and back.
- **Castles:** five castles stand on the shores of the lake: Thun, Hünegg, Oberhofen, Schadau and Spiez. BLS suggests combining a visit with a boat trip; Oberhofen Castle stands right by the lake.
- **Spiez–Faulensee lakeside path:** walk along the shore from Spiez to Faulensee, then take the boat back. From Faulensee to Thun takes about 1 hour.
- **Lake Brienz:** at Interlaken, take the train from Interlaken West to Interlaken Ost for the Lake Brienz boats. BLS's day ticket for both lakes includes that train.

## The boats

BLS's Lake Thun fleet has eight boats: the paddle steamer Blümlisalp, the screw steamer Spiez, and the motor ships Berner Oberland, Stadt Thun, Bubenberg, Beatus, Schilthorn and Stockhorn.

The Blümlisalp is a double-deck saloon steamer from the Belle Époque, built in 1906 by Escher Wyss in Zürich, for up to 750 passengers. After two decades out of service, it was restored between 1989 and 1992 with money raised across Switzerland by the Vaporama cooperative, and it returned to the lake on 22 May 1992. It runs in the summer months. In BLS's published boat plan for 2026, it ran one round trip a day between Thun and Interlaken West from 9 May to 11 October, leaving Thun at 12:40 and Interlaken West at 15:10. BLS notes that the boat plan can change at short notice.

The Spiez, built in 1901, is the oldest steamboat in the fleet. It does not run timetable trips and is currently out of service.

All BLS boats except the small Interlaken, on Lake Brienz, are wheelchair accessible, with level access to the outside decks and the second-class restaurants, and an accessible toilet. On the Berner Oberland and the Stadt Thun, a lift goes up to first class.

## Tickets and passes

On BLS's regular timetable boats, these tickets and passes are valid for free travel, in the class they were bought for:

- GA travelcard
- Swiss Travel Pass, Swiss Travel Pass Flex (with the date entered) and Swiss Transfer Ticket
- Seepass, BLS's annual first-class pass for Lake Thun and Lake Brienz
- Day passes for Half Fare holders, Saver Day Passes, municipal day tickets and children's day passes
- Berner Oberland Pass and Jungfrau Travel Pass
- Eurail and Interrail passes, with the date entered
- Junior Card and Children's Co-travelcard, for children under 16 travelling with the cardholder

The Half Fare travelcard gives 50% off. Children aged 6 to 15 pay half price, and children under 6 travel free. There are no discounts for seniors. The Tell-Pass is not valid. With a second-class pass or ticket, first class needs a supplement, which you can buy at the pier or on board.

BLS's day ticket for both lakes costs CHF 83 in second class and CHF 125 in first class in 2026, or CHF 41.50 and CHF 62.50 with the Half Fare travelcard. It includes the train between Interlaken West and Interlaken Ost and the buses in Interlaken. On your birthday, BLS gives you a free first-class day ticket when you show an identity card or passport.

You can buy tickets on board, from spring to autumn at BLS's ticket offices at the piers in Thun, Spiez and Interlaken West, online in BLS's shop, and at most public transport ticket offices in Switzerland. On board you can pay in Swiss francs or euros, with TWINT or by card. BLS also runs themed cruises, such as fondue and brunch boats; the GA, the Half Fare travelcard and the Seepass are not valid on them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I buy a ticket in Lacus?**
No. Lacus doesn't sell tickets or anything else. You buy your ticket from BLS, the boat operator, online in its shop; Lacus links each trip to BLS's ticket page.

**Do the boats on Lake Thun run in winter?**
Yes. Boats run on Lake Thun all year. In the winter timetable, from 15 November 2026 to 21 March 2027, one boat a day runs each way between Thun and Interlaken West, and on Sundays a brunch boat runs from Thun to Spiez and back. From 13 February to 21 March 2027, short round trips from Thun to Spiez also run from Friday to Sunday. Bikes are not carried from 1 January to 21 March 2027.

**Is the Lake Thun boat included in the Swiss Travel Pass?**
Yes. The Swiss Travel Pass and the Swiss Travel Pass Flex are valid on all regular timetable boats, in the class of the pass.

**Is the boat included in the GA travelcard or the Half Fare travelcard?**
The GA is valid for free travel on all regular boats, in its class. The Half Fare travelcard gives 50% off.

**How long is the boat from Thun to Interlaken?**
About 2 h 10 min from Thun to Interlaken West, with stops at piers along the way. In the summer 2026 timetable there are 6 direct boats a day. In winter there is one.

**How do I get from the Lake Thun boat to the Lake Brienz boat?**
Lake Thun boats end at Interlaken West and Lake Brienz boats start at Interlaken Ost. Take the train between the two stations. BLS's day ticket for both lakes includes it.

**Can I take my dog or my bike on the boat?**
Yes. Dogs under 30 cm at the shoulder travel free in a suitable basket or bag. Other dogs need a dog ticket at half the second-class fare, at most CHF 15 for a day; the SBB dog day pass and dog GA are also valid. Dogs are allowed inside and in the restaurants. Bikes travel if there is space, also at half fare and at most CHF 15 a day; SBB bike day passes and annual bike passes are valid. Bike places cannot be reserved.
