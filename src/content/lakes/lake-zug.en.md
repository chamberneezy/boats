# Lake Zug boat trips and timetable

This guide covers Lake Zug boat trips and the timetable: where the boats go, how long they take, how often they run in summer and in winter, and which tickets and passes are valid. Lake Zug, the Zugersee, lies in central Switzerland between the town of Zug and the foot of the Rigi, and its boats link Zug with Cham, Walchwil, Immensee and Arth on round trips of the lake. Lacus puts every pier and every boat in the official timetable into one search.

## How the boats work

The boats on Lake Zug are run by one company, the Schifffahrtsgesellschaft für den Zugersee AG (SGZ), known as Zugersee Schifffahrt. It belongs to the same group of companies as Zugerland Verkehrsbetriebe, Zug's bus company, and the Zugerberg railway.

The boats run from spring to autumn only. In 2026 the season runs from 12 April to 1 November. Every regular boat is part of a round trip from Zug to Arth am See, at the southern end of the lake, and back, with calls at some of the piers on the way. SGZ publishes these round trips by their times from Zug and back to Zug:

| Round trip from Zug | Monday to Saturday | Sundays and holidays |
|---|---|---|
| 09:45 – 11:55 | 13 April – 17 October | 12 April – 1 November |
| 10:45 – 13:45 | does not run | 10 May – 20 September |
| 12:15 – 14:52 | 13 April – 31 October | 12 April – 1 November |
| 14:00 – 16:45 | does not run | 10 May – 20 September |
| 15:15 – 17:30 | 13 April – 17 October | 12 April – 18 October |

In Zug, the boats leave from the Zug Bahnhofsteg pier, about 400 m from the railway station: take the main exit to Bahnhofplatz and walk straight on towards the lake.

## Classic combinations

- **Rigi:** SGZ suggests taking the boat to Arth, then the train up the Rigi, and hiking back down.
- **Zugerberg:** the mountain above Zug is reached by the Zugerberg railway, which goes up in about eight minutes.
- **Around the lake by bike:** the lake is 42 km around, and SGZ suggests shortening the ride with a boat trip. A bike ticket costs CHF 5 and is valid all day.

## The boats

SGZ's fleet page describes two motor ships:

| Boat | Launched |
|---|---|
| MS Rigi | 20 April 1992 |
| MS Zug | 2 May 2003 |

MS Zug is the flagship. It is 45.6 m long, was built by ÖSWAG in Linz, Austria, and won the iF Design award in 2004. MS Rigi was renovated in 2011 and its interior modernised in 2024. The small MS Schwan, christened in 2001, carries groups of up to 40 people on charter trips.

SGZ's trips are wheelchair accessible, with a wheelchair-accessible toilet on board, and MS Zug has a lift to the upper deck. Of the piers, Pro Infirmis rates Risch and Buonas as less suitable or unsuitable for boarding. Lacus shows the name of the boat for a trip only where the operator publishes it, and for Lake Zug it does not show one.

## Tickets and passes

On regular trips, SGZ accepts the GA travelcard and the Half Fare travelcard, and the Junior Card and the Children's Co-Travelcard are valid. Switzerland Tourism says the Zug–Arth route is fully covered by the Swiss Travel Pass and the Swiss Travel Pass Flex.

Fares in 2026:

- Around-the-lake trip, Zug–Arth–Zug: CHF 43.20, or CHF 21.60 with the Half Fare travelcard and for children aged 6 to 16
- Lake Zug day ticket: CHF 47.00, or CHF 23.50 reduced
- Day pass for public transport and boat, for all zones of the Zug fare network and the boats on Lake Zug and Lake Ägeri: CHF 49.00, or CHF 25.50 reduced. It is not sold on board.
- Children aged 1 to 5 travel free

For a single trip between two piers, you can buy your ticket on board at the route fare; the crew sells tickets after departure. SGZ also sells tickets online. Groups of 10 or more get 30% off on regular trips and must book.

There are no reduced fares on special cruises.

## Special cruises

Besides the timetable, SGZ runs special cruises all year, also in winter when there are no regular boats. Recent examples on its events page include the Lightboat, an Escape game evening, a Fondue Chinoise evening, Sunday brunch buffets, a Santa brunch and the New Year's Eve Cruise with gala dinner. They leave from Zug Bahnhofsteg, and most end at the Zug Schützenmatt pier, about 350 m along the shore. Lacus lists these special cruises, with their dates, times and prices, in the app.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I buy a ticket in Lacus?**
No. Lacus doesn't sell tickets or anything else. You buy your ticket from SGZ, the boat operator, online or on board; Lacus links each trip to SGZ's online ticket shop.

**Do the boats on Lake Zug run in winter?**
No regular boats run in winter. The 2026 season runs from 12 April to 1 November. In winter SGZ runs only special cruises, such as the Lightboat and the New Year's Eve cruise.

**Is the Lake Zug boat included in the Swiss Travel Pass?**
Yes. According to Switzerland Tourism, the Zug–Arth route is fully covered by the Swiss Travel Pass and the Swiss Travel Pass Flex. There are no reduced fares on special cruises.

**Is the boat included in the GA travelcard or the Half Fare travelcard?**
SGZ accepts both on regular trips: the GA for free travel, and the Half Fare travelcard for half price, for example CHF 21.60 instead of CHF 43.20 for the trip around the lake.

**How long is the boat from Zug to Arth?**
About 50 minutes on the 09:45 boat, which goes by Walchwil. The other boats go round by Cham and take about 1 h 15 min to 1 h 40 min.

**Can I buy a ticket on board?**
Yes. Tickets for regular trips are sold on board after departure. The day pass for public transport and boat is not sold on board.

**Can I take my dog on the boat?**
Yes. A dog pays a flat fare of CHF 5, paid on board, and monthly or annual dog passes are accepted. Dogs up to 30 cm at the shoulder travel free in a bag or basket. Dogs must be kept on a lead.

**Can I take my bike on the boat?**
Yes, if there is space. A bike ticket costs CHF 5 and is valid all day, and national bike passes and bike day passes are accepted. Groups with more than five bikes should register the day before.
